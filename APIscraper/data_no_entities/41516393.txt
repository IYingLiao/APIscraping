41516393|t|Integrative Genomic and AI Approaches to Lung Cancer and Implications for Disease Prevention in Former Smokers.
41516393|a|Tobacco smoking accounts for nearly 90% of lung cancer deaths worldwide, yet the mechanisms underlying persistent cancer risk in former smokers are not fully understood. Epidemiological evidence shows that more than 40% of lung cancers develop over 15 years after cessation, demonstrating that while some smoking-induced molecular alterations resolve rapidly, others remain as long-lasting scars that promote carcinogenesis. This review synthesizes longitudinal and cross-sectional genomic, epigenomic, and transcriptomic studies of airway and lung tissues to distinguish persistent from nonpersistent smoking-induced molecular alterations.
